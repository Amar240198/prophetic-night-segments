# Religious language policy

Prophetic Night Segments performs time calculations. It does not issue fatāwā, determine prayer validity, replace qualified scholars, or independently determine prayer times.

## Required distinctions

- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 1131 is summarised as the Dāwūd night pattern: half sleep, one third prayer, then one sixth sleep. The six-part mapping is mathematical.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 1145 concerns the excellence of the prayer and fasting associated with Dāwūd and may be referenced without turning it into an obligation.
- Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī 1146 is summarised as the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ sleeping earlier, praying in the latter part, returning to bed, then rising at the adhān.
- The last reference supports the general sleep–pray–sleep–Fajr structure. It does not prove that the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ always used six exact portions or always rose at B3.
- The exact six-part visual maps directly to the separately narrated Dāwūd pattern.

Never call optional alarms required, call the schedule compulsory, describe the tool as a religious ruling, or call Parts 4–5 the last third. The mathematical last third is Parts 5–6.

References should use identifiers and concise summaries rather than long translations. Product copy must separate direct narration, mathematical mapping, and personal scheduling choices.
